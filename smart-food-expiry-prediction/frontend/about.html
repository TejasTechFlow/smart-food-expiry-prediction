About

What it does

Predicts the remaining shelf life of foods from storage conditions, saves items to an inventory and tracks their freshness status.

Why it is useful

Households throw away a lot of edible food. Knowing roughly when something will go off helps plan meals and reduce waste.

How machine learning is used

A Random Forest Regressor inside a scikit-learn Pipeline (one-hot encoding + model) predicts total shelf life in days. Remaining life = total − days already stored; expiry date = purchase date + total shelf life. Status: Fresh (>7 days left), Use Soon (3–7), Near Estimated Expiry (1–2), Estimated Shelf Life Exceeded (0 or less).

Technologies

HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Bootstrap 5, Chart.js, FastAPI, SQLAlchemy + SQLite, pandas, scikit-learn, Joblib.

Main features

ML shelf-life prediction, dashboard charts, inventory management, persistent prediction history with CSV export.

Limitations
  • The training data is synthetic (generated from assumed rules), not real experiments.
  • It ignores microbial contamination, handling and temperature fluctuations.
  • Only six input features are used.
Future improvements
  • Train on real lab or industry shelf-life data.
  • Barcode scanning and reminder notifications.
  • Per-food (not only per-category) models.
